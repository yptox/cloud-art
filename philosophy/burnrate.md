# BURNRATE

> *An algorithmic philosophy on the material debt of frictionless abundance, paid in watts, water, and the heat of a thumb.*

## The Movement

**BURNRATE** names the defining cultural condition of the generative era: provenance collapse. Generation is free; verification is expensive. When any image can be synthesized instantly, scarcity does not disappear — it migrates. It leaves the content and settles in trust, witness, and material cost. Culture bifurcates into two economies: an infinite, frictionless synthetic surface, and a renewed premium on the provably costly — the live, the physical, the energy-burning, the human-witnessed.

The piece sits exactly on that seam. It is a feed indistinguishable from any content platform: beautiful, endless, machine-dreamed imagery, with no irony at the surface. It must genuinely seduce, because the seduction is the mechanism. But every image chars, melts, and heat-distorts in proportion to its estimated generation cost, and a small bureaucratic ledger accumulates what the beauty spent: watt-hours, millilitres of cooling water, GPU-seconds, and the coordinates of the data center that paid. The internet no longer has an author — but it still has a body, and the body pays.

The scroll gesture is the ignition source. This is the piece's only argument, and it is made in the viewer's own hand: beauty and expenditure are one gesture. The fire cannot be reversed; the ledger never resets; the feed never ends. Refusal to scroll is the only ethical act available inside the work — and the work is designed so that you scroll anyway.

## The Computation

The renderer is three.js carrying two custom GLSL passes. The first produces each "photograph" — the feed ships as a pre-generated batch of seeded, deterministic compositions (dusk gradients, dunes, cloudscapes, marbled fluids, nebulae, silk) built from domain-warped fractal noise and curated cosine palettes, finished with tone curve, vignette, and grain so the surface reads editorial, sold; where the batch is unreachable, the same shader synthesizes the same sequence live. The second pass is the combustion, and the only heavy per-frame work. The burn is signed-distance edge erosion: every pixel knows its distance to the image's border, and a front sweeps through that field, displaced by a noise-advected mask so the fire wanders like a behavior instead of closing like a shape. Ahead of the front, emissive cracks preheat through the intact image; at the front, a white-hot rim flickers on a blackbody ramp; behind it, a char band crumbles into transparency, ember particles lift off on their own updraft, and the pristine next image is revealed through heat-haze refraction — sampled through a refracting offset so the air above the fire genuinely swims. The destruction is physically grounded, not glitch-art: char, ember, ash, haze.

Scroll input feeds the front directly, and burning is monotonic — there is no un-burning, and past a small ignition threshold the fire self-sustains, because fires do. Each image's cost metadata is derived deterministically from its index: model class, watt-hours scaled from published per-query measurements (Luccioni, Jernite & Strubell, 2023), water drawn through site-specific L/kWh intensities (Li, Yang, Islam & Ren, 2023), coordinates from published hyperscale sites. Costlier images burn hotter and longer. The estimates are the point: the figures are real in magnitude and deliberately obscured by the providers who could publish them precisely.

## The Discipline

The piece does not lecture. There is no end-card, no manifesto, no moment where the surface breaks character to explain itself. The ledger fades in only after ten images — after the viewer is already complicit — and it speaks in the flattest register available: an invoice. The final total is written to the page title, so the browser tab itself becomes a receipt for the session's consumption.

There is a further honesty in the piece's construction: the batch is generated by the same shader that serves as the feed's fallback, so wherever the pre-rendered images cannot reach the viewer, the imagery synthesizes live on the viewer's own GPU — and the burn pass always runs there. The watts on the page are partly drawn from the device in the viewer's hand; the warmth of the phone is the artwork's only physical edition. The feed is deterministic and shared — every viewer burns the same endless sequence — but each act of burning is unrepeatable, because the ledger only ever goes up. What the work disciplines is not the machine but the gesture: the cheapest movement a body can make, revealed as the most expensive thing on the page.
