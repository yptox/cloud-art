# Dispatch: BURNRATE

*~500 words, for the publication.*

---

There is a moment, about ten images deep, when BURNRATE stops being a feed and becomes an invoice. Until then it behaves exactly like every other surface you scroll: full-bleed, lush, endless, machine-dreamed. No chrome, no explanation, no irony. It has to seduce, because the seduction is the mechanism.

But each image you scroll past ignites. Not a transition, not a dissolve — a fire. The edges char first, a signed-distance erosion eating inward under a noise-driven mask; cracks glow through the surface before it goes; a white-hot rim closes toward the centre; the air above the burn actually refracts. And the fire is priced. Every image carries metadata — model class, inference seconds, estimated watt-hours, millilitres of cooling water, the coordinates of the data centre that paid — and burns in proportion to what its generation is estimated to have cost. A costlier image burns hotter and longer. When the small monospace ledger finally fades in, bottom-left, bureaucratic as a utility bill, you are already complicit: energy, water, compute, images. The total is written into the page title, so the browser tab itself becomes your receipt. Closing it is the only exit.

The premise underneath is what I've started calling provenance collapse. Generation is free; verification is expensive. When any image can be synthesized instantly, scarcity doesn't vanish — it migrates out of content and into trust, witness, and material cost. Culture is splitting into two economies: an infinite frictionless synthetic surface, and a renewed premium on the provably costly — the live, the physical, the energy-burning, the human-witnessed. My earlier work argued that the internet has a body: cables, warehouses, heat. BURNRATE extends the claim into the generative era. The internet no longer has an author. But it still has a body, and the body pays.

The numbers are estimates, and that is the point. The energy figures scale from published per-query measurements (Luccioni, Jernite and Strubell's ~2.9 Wh mean per generated image); the water from published litres-per-kilowatt-hour intensities that vary wildly by site — Finland sips, Texas gulps. Providers could publish precise per-query figures. They don't. So the piece foregrounds its own estimation the way a utility bill foregrounds its meter readings: these costs are real in magnitude and deliberately obscured in detail.

I want to be honest about what the piece refuses to do. It does not lecture about slop. There is no end-card, no moment where the surface breaks character to explain itself. The argument is made entirely in your hand: beauty and expenditure revealed as one gesture. The fire cannot be reversed — there is no un-burning, and past a small threshold it self-sustains, because fires do. The ledger never resets. The feed never ends. Refusal to scroll is the only ethical act available inside the work, and the work is designed, with everything I know about how feeds are designed, so that you scroll anyway.

That design knowledge is the uncomfortable part, and I've kept it in deliberately. The piece is not above the economy it describes. It burns because you want to see what's underneath. So did I.

*BURNRATE runs in any modern browser. The ledger does not reset.*
